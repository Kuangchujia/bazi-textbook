# Lesson 5 · Relations among the Earthly Branches

[简体中文](/textbook/L05/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L05/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L05/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L05/)

## 1. The six harmonies

> Lay the twelve branches out in order and fold the line in the middle: what lies above and below a fold is a pair in harmony.

Chou and zi, yin and hai, mao and xu, chen and you, si and shen, wu and wei: six pairs. They divide into two kinds.

**Generating pairs**, where one gives and the other gains:

- yin and hai combine into wood, since hai water generates yin wood.
- chen and you combine into metal, since chen earth generates you metal.
- wu and wei combine into earth, since wu fire generates wei earth.

**Checking pairs**, where the two pull against one another and both lose:

- zi and chou combine into earth, since chou earth checks zi water.
- mao and xu combine into fire, since mao wood checks xu earth.
- si and shen combine into water, since si fire checks shen metal.

> Note. The beginner’s class uses binding only; transformation belongs to the advanced course.
> **A gap breaks the relation.** The branches are still by nature and wait to be used. A harmony takes effect only where the two stand next to each other in the original chart. If another branch lies between them — a year branch of zi, a month branch of yin, a day branch of chou — then zi and chou are a pair on paper, but with yin between them the gap breaks the relation and no harmony is counted.

## 2. The six clashes

> The same fold, from a different starting point: fold at the middle of the line.

Zi and wu, chou and wei, yin and shen, mao and you, chen and xu, si and hai: six clashes. A clash is opposite direction together with a phase that checks, and falls into three degrees.

**Water against fire, the sharpest:** zi and wu, water of due north against fire of due south; si and hai, fire of the south-east against water of the north-west.

**Metal against wood, next:** yin and shen, wood of the north-east against metal of the south-west; mao and you, wood of due east against metal of due west.

**Earth against earth, dry against damp:** chou and wei, damp earth of the north-east against dry earth of the south-west; chen and xu, damp earth of the south-east against dry earth of the north-west.

> Harmony folds between chou and yin, clash folds between zi and wu: one method, a different alignment.
> From the *Yuanhai Ziping* on the clash: zi, wu, mao and you are clashes of the four centres, their qi single and their force fierce; yin, shen, si and hai are clashes of the four beginnings, their qi mixed and changeable; chen, xu, chou and wei are clashes of the four stores, earth against earth, dry against damp. The nature of a clash is opposite direction with a checking phase, like two men meeting head on: the greater the force, the harder the impact.
> **A gap breaks the relation.** As with the harmonies, a clash needs the two branches side by side. With zi in the year, yin in the month and wu in the day, zi and wu are a pair on paper, but the yin between them stops the clash.

What a clash does in practice. Two equal forces meet and neither gives way, and both are damaged. A clash moves, and movement brings change: a fortunate god clashed scatters its good, an unlucky god clashed releases its harm. A chart with a clash in it is unsettled for life; a clash arriving with the cycles brings a change in that year. In marriage it shows plainly: the palace of the spouse clashed usually means two strong tempers, neither yielding, quarrels that go on, and a pile of small wrongs that will not come straight.

## 3. The six harms

> A harm is the hidden injury left behind when a harmony is broken apart.

The logic of how they form: a harmony is first broken by a clash, and the harm comes out of the breaking. Zi and chou are a pair, and wei clashes chou, leaving zi harming wei. Wu and wei are a pair, and zi clashes wu, leaving chou harming wu.

Hence six pairs:

- zi harms wei: zi water pierces wei earth, water getting in and being trapped.
- chou harms wu: chou earth dulls wu fire, fire covered by earth.
- yin harms si: yin wood generates si fire, and the harm comes inside the generating.
- mao harms chen: mao wood checks chen earth, wood against earth.
- shen harms hai: shen metal generates hai water, and the harm comes inside the generating.
- you harms xu: you metal drains xu earth, metal against earth.

What a harm does in practice. A harm works in the dark, and its damage goes further than a clash: a clash is an open quarrel, a harm a secret undermining. It points to damage done out of sight, to talk and trouble, to being worked against by someone behind one’s back, and it is used to read the undercurrents in a relationship. The *Yuanhai Ziping* says that a harm is a piercing: a harmony meeting a clash gives birth to a harm, and every harm comes of a harmony broken up. Where the palace of marriage meets a harm, it often means separation or loss, the structure of a household broken, which is graver than a clash.

> **A gap breaks the relation.** As with harmonies and clashes, the two must stand next to each other; a gap and no harm is counted.

## 4. The triple harmonies

> Three branches come together in the rhythm of growth, peak and tomb, and make the strongest combination there is.

| Triple harmony | Growth | Peak | Tomb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shen, zi, chen to water | shen | zi | chen |
| hai, mao, wei to wood | hai | mao | wei |
| yin, wu, xu to fire | yin | wu | xu |
| si, you, chou to metal | si | you | chou |

> The mnemonic: shen, zi and chen water; hai, mao and wei wood; yin, wu and xu fire; si, you and chou metal.

On the conditions for transformation. Given the present state of the course, those are set out in the advanced class. What is wanted here is the make-up of the triple harmony, and the two partial forms.

> **Position does not matter here.** Unlike the harmonies, clashes and harms, which need the two branches side by side, a triple harmony forms as soon as all three characters are present, in whatever order they stand.

### Partial harmonies and arched harmonies

Three together is the ideal and two the usual case. By which one is missing there are three forms.

**The growth-side half**, growth and peak without the tomb:

- shen and zi half to water; hai and mao half to wood; yin and wu half to fire; si and you half to metal.

**The tomb-side half**, peak and tomb without the growth:

- zi and chen half to water; mao and wei half to wood; wu and xu half to fire; you and chou half to metal.

**The arch**, growth and tomb without the middle. This one differs from the other two: the two must stand side by side, and with the middle missing they arch over an invisible middle branch:

- hai and wei side by side arch over mao; shen and chen over zi; si and chou over you; yin and xu over wu.

What the arch means: the middle branch is not present in the chart, and its force still acts on it. Note, though, that the arched branch does not take part in the generation and check of the chart. Not taking part is not the same as not existing; its hidden pull is still weighed.

Given a ten god, an arched branch shows its character as hidden: not in the open, and working all the same. By ten god:

**Arching the officer** (a direct officer): a hidden patron, hidden work, a hidden husband in a woman’s chart, hidden injury, a hidden petty man, hidden children in a man’s.

**Arching the killing** (seven killings): a hidden petty man, a hidden lover in a woman’s chart, hidden illness, hidden trouble with the law, hidden children in a man’s.

**Arching the seal** (direct or indirect): a hidden patron, a hidden house, hidden papers, contracts and evidence.

**Arching the peer** (same-rank or rob wealth): a hidden partner who helps, a hidden competitor, a hidden assistant, a hidden drain on money.

**Arching food or wounding** (eating god or wounding officer): hidden skill and talent, hidden income, hidden children in a woman’s chart.

**Arching wealth** (direct or indirect): hidden income, a hidden lover in a man’s chart.

> The arch is about what is hidden: not on the surface of the chart and pulling on it all the same. Where a chart has one, its hidden force has to be weighed.

## 5. The meetings

> Three branches of the same direction gathered together collect the full strength of that quarter, by season.

- yin, mao and chen: the wood of the east, spring.
- si, wu and wei: the fire of the south, summer.
- shen, you and xu: the metal of the west, autumn.
- hai, zi and chou: the water of the north, winter.

Unlike a triple harmony, a meeting does not run on growth, peak and tomb. It collects qi by direction alone: yin, mao and chen are all spring wood, and met together the wood is doubled.

> Order of force: a meeting is stronger than a triple harmony, and a triple harmony stronger than a pair in harmony. A meeting is also the hardest to have, since all three of one direction are needed; a triple harmony can be half or arched.

## 6. The three punishments

> Punishment is the obscurest part of the relations among branches, and is used mostly for trouble and disaster.

Four groups:

- **yin, si and shen**, the punishment of ingratitude: kindness returned with enmity, being betrayed. Bloodshed, sudden illness, trouble with the law and prison.
- **chou, xu and wei**, the punishment of relying on power: the strong oppressing the weak, quarrels among equals. Falls and knocks, illness.
- **zi and mao**, the punishment without propriety: the child punishing the parent, the junior overriding the senior, courtesy broken. Common between mother and son, teacher and pupil, senior and junior.
- **chen, wu, you and hai**, self-punishment: a man at odds with himself. Chen suppressed, wu hasty, you perfectionist, hai tangled inside.

> Punishment belongs to fine-grained reading. The beginner should master the harmonies, clashes, harms, triple harmonies and meetings first, and leave punishment and breaking until there is experience.

## 7. The breakings

> A breaking is a split: a small matter made large, a small gap turned into a crack.

Six pairs: zi and you, yin and hai, chen and chou, wu and mao, shen and si, xu and wei.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. Punishment and breaking belong to fine reading. Master the harmonies, clashes, harms, triple harmonies and meetings first; leave these two until experience has grown.

## Summary of the lesson

1. The six harmonies: zi and chou, yin and hai, mao and xu, chen and you, si and shen, wu and wei.
2. The six clashes: zi and wu, chou and wei, yin and shen, mao and you, chen and xu, si and hai.
3. The six harms: made when a harmony is clashed apart, and working in the dark.
4. The triple harmonies: growth, peak and tomb gathered, the strongest combination among branches.
5. The meetings: three of one direction, stronger still than a triple harmony.
6. The three punishments: ingratitude, reliance on power, without propriety, and self-punishment; fine reading.
7. The breakings: small gaps turned to cracks; enough to know they exist.

## Practice

1. Write out the six harmonies from memory, marking which generate and which check.
2. Write out the six clashes.
3. Write out the four triple harmonies with their growth, peak and tomb.
4. Write out the four meetings.
5. In a chart, yin and shen clash; yin is the lu of a jia wood day master and shen the lu of geng metal, the seven killings. What does that clash mean for the working life?
6. In the si, you and chou triple harmony of metal, why can si, which is fire, take part in making metal?
