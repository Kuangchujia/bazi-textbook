# Lesson 7 · The Twelve Stages

[简体中文](/textbook/L07/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L07/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L07/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L07/)

## 1. What the twelve stages are

From the *Siyan Dubu*, on advance and retreat:

> 进气退气，命物相争；进气不死，退气不生。
> Qi coming in and qi going out, fate and things contending: what is coming in does not die, what is going out does not live.

And the *Wuyan Dubu* on the same point:

> 进气死不死，退气生不生；终年无发旺，犹忌少年刑。
> What is coming in, though dead, does not die; what is going out, though living, does not live; a life with no flourishing at all still fears the punishments of its youth.

The twelve stages are exactly this: qi coming in and qi going out. A stem on the incoming line, from growth to the peak, is not ill even when weak, since life is held in reserve; a stem on the outgoing line, from decline to extinction, does not last even when strong, since its force is running out. To know a stem’s state, see which line it is on; that matters more than its absolute strength.

### 1.1 The phases through the seasons

The phases are born of the four seasons and are strong when in command:

- **Spring, wood in command:** wood generates fire, so fire is next; wood relies on water, so water rests; fire checks metal, so metal is imprisoned; wood checks earth, so earth dies.
- **Summer, fire in command:** fire generates earth, so earth is next; fire relies on wood, so wood rests; water checks fire, so water is imprisoned; fire checks metal, so metal dies.
- **The sixth month, earth in command:** earth generates metal, so metal is next; earth relies on fire, so fire rests; wood checks earth, so wood is imprisoned; earth checks water, so water dies.
- **Autumn, metal in command:** metal generates water, so water is next; metal relies on earth, so earth rests; fire checks metal, so fire is imprisoned; metal checks wood, so wood dies.
- **Winter, water in command:** water generates wood, so wood is next; water relies on metal, so metal rests; earth checks water, so earth is imprisoned; water checks fire, so fire dies.

> The rule behind it: what reaches its height declines, what has done its work withdraws, and at the yang extreme the yin is born. The sun past noon leans west, the moon full begins to wane: the way of heaven, and the way of the phases.

From the *Yuanhai Ziping*:

> 不可专泥于生旺而吉、衰败而凶，当以活法推之。
> Do not be fixed on growth and strength as fortune and decline as misfortune; argue it as a living thing.

The twelve stages are not a plain rule that strong is good and weak is bad. A body at the peak with nothing to control it can come to disaster, and a body in a declining place with support can still prosper. They are not labels but a reading of the channel the energy runs in.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The great way is simple. What is taught here is the groundwork, and the reading of charts later uses the same thing: strength and decline, advance and retreat, qi coming in and going out. Without that ground, the ten gods, however well learned, are a castle in the air.

### 1.2 Where the twelve come from

The *Kaoyuan* says: wood grows in hai, fire in yin, metal in si, water in shen, and earth too grows in shen, lodged in yin.

Each runs through growth, bathing, capping, office, peak, decline, illness, death, tomb, extinction, conception and nurture, through twelve positions in order. The way of heaven goes round and gives birth without end.

The ten stems are lodged in the twelve stages in one unbroken round, much as a human life runs its course and begins again.

> On the growth of earth: wu earth grows in yin and also in shen, and the two do not contradict each other. Writers on choosing auspicious days mostly use shen, since shen is kun, kun is earth, and water and earth congeal there; writers on the several arts of prediction mostly use yin, since yin is the first month of spring, when the qi of heaven descends and the qi of earth rises and grass and trees begin to stir. This course follows the arts of prediction and takes yin as the growth of wu earth. Both readings stand, each with its ground.

The *Xiji Pian*, on finding life at the point of extinction:

> 五行绝处，即是胎元，生日逢之，名曰受气。
> Where the five phases are extinct is the origin of conception; a birthday meeting it is called receiving qi.

The finest thing in the twelve is the round itself: at extinction stands conception, and death carries new life. That is not pessimism but the teaching of the great ultimate: at the yin extreme the yang is born, and death and life are not two. Growth must lead to the peak, the peak must fall to decline and extinction, and at extinction a single yang returns and the round enters conception and nurture again. One turn of the twelve is one whole life.

> Death, tomb, extinction, conception, nurture: each has its place and each has its charge. The beginner’s technique holds more than it seems, and practice is where it comes out.

## 2. The twelve, one by one

### 2.1 What each stage means

The twelve are the twelve stages of a life from birth to death:

**Growth**, strong: things sprouting, as a child is born. All that is new beginning, new opening, new life coming up.

**Bathing**, weak, the place of ruin: the infant washed clean. Also called the pool, and it governs attraction and emotional entanglement; its force is not yet steady.

**Capping**, strong: things growing fine, the child dressed and capped. Out of naked weakness and into a social part.

**Office**, strong, also called lu: grown to adulthood and able to lead. Work accomplished, capacity ripe, standing out in front.

**Peak**, strong: full to the extreme, able to assist a ruler. The height has come, and from the height comes decline.

**Decline**, ordinary: the height passed and the turn downward. Not a fall, but the momentum reversing.

**Illness**, ordinary: as a person falls sick, all things flag. Force plainly receding, and rest and treatment wanted.

**Death**, weak: qi spent and form gone, the lowest point before the bottom.

**Tomb**, weak, also called the store: dead and laid in the tomb, the force sealed and put away. An end, and a reserve.

**Extinction**, weak: the qi before is cut off and the qi after has not come. Utterly empty, and the eve of a single returning yang.

**Conception**, strong, new life: the qi of the parents gathered into a seed. Born at the point of extinction, holding life without limit.

**Nurture**, strong, new life: the seed fed in the womb, about to be born. Strength gathered, and a new round about to begin.

> One turn of the twelve is one whole life: at extinction stands conception, and death carries new life. What is learned is not twelve labels but the whole channel by which energy rises and falls, dies and is born.

## 3. The balance of force

For the reading of fortune the twelve fall into three camps, which give the day master its ground colour in the branch where it stands:

**The four strong** — growth, capping, office, peak. Force in its rise, energy in plenty.

**The four bad** — bathing, death, tomb, extinction. Energy low or sealed away. Bathing is also called the place of ruin and the pool, and governs attraction and emotional instability; it belongs with the bad, though it is not the trough that death, tomb and extinction are.

**The four ordinary** — decline, illness, conception, nurture. A plain passage, neither strong nor bad, gathering for a change.

That is the base. What matters more is the channel behind it: growth to peak is qi coming in, energy climbing; decline to nurture is qi going out, energy receding. Which channel a stem stands in matters more than its strength on its own: on the incoming line it is not ill though weak, on the outgoing line it does not last though strong. See the four images in lesson 2.

> All of this is description in general; the application is in practice. Given the present frame of the course, the detail belongs to the advanced class.

## 4. The ten stems in the twelve

### 4.1 The rule for the yang stems

Yang advances, and takes advancing as its advance. A yang stem grows where its phase is generated, that is, in the branch of what generates it:

| Stem | Growth | Peak | Tomb | Death |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| jia | hai | mao | wei | wu |
| bing | yin | wu | xu | you |
| wu | yin | wu | xu | you |
| geng | si | you | chou | zi |
| ren | shen | zi | chen | mao |

> The rule: a yang stem grows in what generates it. Hai generates jia, so hai is the growth of jia; yin generates bing and wu; si generates geng; shen generates ren.

### 4.2 The rule for the yin stems

Yin goes against, and takes retreating as its advance. Where the yang dies the yin is born, so a yin stem grows where the corresponding yang stem dies:

| Stem | Growth | Peak | Tomb | Death |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yi | wu | yin | xu | hai |
| ding | you | si | chou | yin |
| ji | you | si | chou | yin |
| xin | zi | shen | chen | si |
| gui | mao | hai | wei | shen |

> The rule: a yin stem grows in what it generates. Yi generates wu, so wu is the growth of yi; ding generates you; xin generates zi; gui generates mao.

### 4.3 The full table

| Stem | Grow | Bathe | Cap | Office | Peak | Decline | Ill | Death | Tomb | Extinct | Conceive | Nurture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| jia wood | hai | zi | chou | yin | mao | chen | si | wu | wei | shen | you | xu |
| yi wood | wu | si | chen | mao | yin | chou | zi | hai | xu | you | shen | wei |
| bing fire | yin | mao | chen | si | wu | wei | shen | you | xu | hai | zi | chou |
| ding fire | you | shen | wei | wu | si | chen | mao | yin | chou | zi | hai | xu |
| wu earth | yin | mao | chen | si | wu | wei | shen | you | xu | hai | zi | chou |
| ji earth | you | shen | wei | wu | si | chen | mao | yin | chou | zi | hai | xu |
| geng metal | si | wu | wei | shen | you | xu | hai | zi | chou | yin | mao | chen |
| xin metal | zi | hai | xu | you | shen | wei | wu | si | chen | mao | yin | chou |
| ren water | shen | you | xu | hai | zi | chou | yin | mao | chen | si | wu | wei |
| gui water | mao | yin | chou | zi | hai | xu | you | shen | wei | wu | si | chen |

> The mnemonic: jia in hai, yi in wu, bing and wu in yin; ding and ji in you; geng in si, xin in zi, ren in shen; gui water grows in mao. Yang stems run forward through the twelve, yin stems back from the place of death. The four growths fall in yin, shen, si and hai; the four peaks in zi, wu, mao and you.

### 4.4 The four growths, peaks and stores

One pattern shows in the table: the growth, peak and tomb of every stem fall on only eight branches.

**The four growths**, yin, shen, si and hai: where the yang stems grow, the gates and sources of the phases.

**The four peaks**, zi, wu, mao and you: where the yin stems grow, the extremes of the phases.

**The four stores**, chen for water, xu for fire, chou for metal and wei for wood: where the phases are kept and come to rest.

> These eight branches carry great weight in a chart: they hold the three key points of a stem’s energy, from birth through the peak to the store.
> A summary from Chujia Kuang. The heart of the twelve is yang forward and yin backward: the yang stems run with the order, the yin stems against it, each the mirror of the other. Twelve stages, each with its own meaning for energy. Hold the one switch of yang forward and yin backward and the twelve fall into place.

## Summary of the lesson

1. Where the twelve come from: the way of heaven going round, twelve stages like the stages of a life.
2. The twelve in order: growth, bathing, capping, office, peak, decline, illness, death, tomb, extinction, conception, nurture.
3. The balance of force: the four strong, the four bad, the four ordinary.
4. The yang stems: yang advances, and grows where its phase is generated — jia in hai, bing and wu in yin, geng in si, ren in shen.
5. The yin stems: yin retreats, and grows where it generates — yi in wu, ding and ji in you, xin in zi, gui in mao.
6. The four growths yin, shen, si and hai; the four peaks zi, wu, mao and you; the four stores chen, xu, chou and wei.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: the order

What is the correct order of the twelve stages?

1. growth, bathing, capping, office, peak, decline, illness, death, tomb, extinction, conception, nurture
2. growth, capping, bathing, office, peak, decline, illness, death, tomb, extinction, conception, nurture
3. growth, bathing, capping, office, peak, decline, illness, death, extinction, tomb, conception, nurture
4. growth, bathing, capping, peak, office, decline, illness, death, tomb, extinction, conception, nurture

???+ "Answer"
    A. From birth to death and to new birth, round without end.

### Exercise 2: a yang stem

Where does jia wood grow?

1. yin
2. si
3. shen
4. hai

???+ "Answer"
    D. A yang stem grows where its phase is generated, that is, in the branch of its mother: jia in hai, since water generates wood; bing in yin; wu in yin, lodged there; geng in si; ren in shen. The mnemonic: jia hai, bing yin, wu yin, geng si, ren shen.

### Exercise 3: a yin stem

Where does xin metal grow?

1. wu
2. you
3. zi
4. mao

???+ "Answer"
    C. A yin stem grows where it generates, retreating as its advance and standing opposite the death of its yang: xin in zi, since metal generates water; yi in wu; ding in you; ji in you; gui in mao. The mnemonic: yi wu, ding you, ji you, xin zi, gui mao.

### Exercise 4: the three camps

Which group belongs to the four bad?

1. growth, capping, office, peak
2. bathing, death, tomb, extinction
3. decline, illness, conception, nurture
4. growth, bathing, capping, office

???+ "Answer"
    B. The four strong are growth, capping, office and peak; the four bad are bathing, death, tomb and extinction; the four ordinary are decline, illness, conception and nurture.

### Exercise 5: in a chart

A day master of jia wood with the month branch hai: in what state does the jia stand, and what does it mean?

1. bathing: attraction and entanglement
2. growth: strong life, a new beginning
3. peak: the height of a career
4. tomb: force sealed away

???+ "Answer"
    B. Jia wood grows in hai, so the day master is said to have found growth in the month: strong vitality, as a person born into good conditions and a good starting point. In the same way jia in wu is at death, the weakest, and jia in mao at the peak, the strongest. In practice, a day master at growth is a good sign, of vitality and life; one at death, tomb or extinction wants rest and gathering.
