# Lesson 2 · Yin and Yang, the Five Phases

[简体中文](/textbook/L02/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L02/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L02/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L02/)

## 2.1 Yin and yang

### 2.1.1 What yin and yang are

The phrase comes from the *Yijing*: “one yin and one yang, that is the way.”

The two words look esoteric and are taken from ordinary life. Where the sun reaches, warm and bright, is yang; where it does not, cold and dark, is yin. Moving and giving outwards is yang; settling and drawing inwards is yin. Yin stands for the passive, the inward, the dark, the cold, the soft, the still; yang for the active, the outward, the bright, the warm, the hard, the moving. Once that everyday sense is in hand, the yin and yang of a chart stops feeling abstract.

On the diagram of the great ultimate, white is yang and black is yin. What matters is not the division but the dot: black in the white and white in the black, yin inside yang and yang inside yin, each holding the other. Every ten god in a chart is read with that in mind.

Heaven is yang and earth yin; the sun yang and the moon yin; man yang and woman yin; heat yang and cold yin; movement yang and rest yin. Yin and yang are everywhere, and anything can be divided by them.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. Yin and yang are two directions of one and the same qi: yang dispersing, yin gathering. Charts are divided into yin and yang stems and branches so that one can see whether the force is going out or drawing in. One body, two faces, wherever you look: the direct officer is the root of honour and also the lock on the person; the direct seal is the source that feeds the self and can also take the eating god away; the wealth star feeds a life and can also break the seal; the wounding officer damages the officer and is also the star of the great writer. The same thing, seen from another side, is another thing.

### 2.1.2 Four rules

Yin and yang are not two fixed labels but a working system. Four rules take the student from memorising the lists to seeing how the two behave.

**They are one body.** Everything holds both sides and cannot be cut in two. Day and night come together; without one there is no other.

**Each is the root of the other.** There is yang inside yin and yin inside yang. At the summer solstice a little yin is born; at the winter solstice a little yang.

**They wax and wane.** As one grows the other recedes, in a moving balance: spring and summer, autumn and winter, cold going and heat coming, round without end.

**They turn into one another.** Given the conditions, each becomes the other. The sun past noon declines; the moon full begins to wane.

From the *Yuanhai Ziping*:

> 冬至一阳生，则有木火生旺之理；夏至一阴生，则有金生水用之理。
> At the winter solstice a single yang is born, and there is the principle by which wood and fire come to strength; at the summer solstice a single yin is born, and there is the principle by which metal generates water and is put to use.

### 2.1.3 The division in a chart

| Earthly branches | zi, yin, chen, wu, shen, xu | chou, mao, si, wei, you, hai |

## 2.2 The four images

### 2.2.1 From two forms to five phases

From the *Ziping Zhenquan*:

> 有老少，遂分四象。老者极动极静之时，是为太阳太阴；少者初动初静之际，是为少阴少阳。有是四象，而五行具于其中矣。水者，太阴也；火者，太阳也；木者，少阳也；金者，少阴也；土者，阴阳老少、木火金水冲气所结也。
> There is the old and the young, and so the four images divide. The old is the extreme of movement and of stillness, the greater yang and the greater yin; the young is the first movement and the first stillness, the lesser yin and the lesser yang. With these four the five phases are all present. Water is the greater yin, fire the greater yang, wood the lesser yang, metal the lesser yin; earth is what the clash of the old and young, of wood, fire, metal and water, congeals.

The chain is not complicated. The great ultimate is a mass of undivided qi; the first movement divides it into yin and yang, the two forms. The division is not a cut into halves: it is one qi showing four states as it waxes and wanes. Just emerging and faintly rising is lesser yang — wood, spring, east, green. Grown to its height and fullest is greater yang — fire, summer, south, red. Past the height and beginning to gather is lesser yin — metal, autumn, west, white. Gathered to the extreme and sunk to the bottom is greater yin — water, winter, north, black. What the clash of the four congeals is earth, which sits at the centre and flourishes at the end of each season. That is the whole path from the ultimate to the two forms, the four images and the five phases.

> 太极（混沌一气）→ 两仪（分阴分阳）→ 四象（少阳·太阳·少阴·太阴）→ 五行（木·火·土·金·水）
> The great ultimate, one undivided qi; the two forms, yin and yang; the four images, lesser yang, greater yang, lesser yin, greater yin; the five phases, wood, fire, earth, metal, water.

### 2.2.2 The four images as a model of growth

The four images are not only the seasons of the natural world. They are a general model for a human life and for anything that begins, grows and ends.

**Lesser yang.** Yang rising, yin receding. Youth in a person, the start of an undertaking, the first growth of things: everything is still open.

**Greater yang.** Yang at its height, yin beginning. The prime of life, the height of an undertaking, the fullness of things. The peak has arrived, and with it the hidden trouble of the peak.

**Lesser yin.** Yin growing, yang receding. Age, decline, things falling away. The turn from fullness to decline, when one should draw in and wait.

**Greater yin.** Yin at its height, yang beginning. The end of a life, the close of an undertaking, things returning to store. Yin at the extreme gives birth to yang; a single yang comes back, and a new round begins.

Birth, age, sickness and death; the arising, lasting, decaying and emptying of an undertaking; the rise and fall of a trade: all of it stays inside this round, which cannot be cut anywhere. The strength and decline of the phases in a chart, the rise and fall of the ten gods, the turning of the cycles, are this round seen at different points.

What is to be learned here is the word cycle. The untutored instinct says that greater yang, the strongest state, is the best; but what reaches an extreme turns. After greater yang comes lesser yin, the turn from fullness to decline, which is the path from the peak through decline, illness, death and the tomb. Lesser yang is weaker than greater yang, standing only at birth, and it is on the way up, with the peak still ahead. Hence the old advice to hold fullness and keep it safe: better a steady rise than the peak.

### 2.2.3 Earth and the four transition months

Wood, fire, metal and water each have a season. Earth alone has none of its own. It flourishes at the end of each season, in the four months of transition:

**Chen**, the third month: late spring into early summer, wood ending and fire arriving. **Wei**, the sixth: late summer into early autumn, fire ending and metal arriving. **Xu**, the ninth: late autumn into early winter, metal ending and water arriving. **Chou**, the twelfth: late winter into early spring, water ending and wood arriving.

The *Ziping Zhenquan* calls earth what the clash of the four congeals. In chen, xu, chou and wei, earth carries, mediates and brings to birth: one qi is ending, the next is beginning, and the junction is the point of change. For charts born in those months, the strength of earth needs particular attention.

> A study note. The working use of the four images — judging the strength of the phases and choosing the useful god — comes after the generation and check of the phases (2.4) and the theory of strength and decline (lesson 12). What is wanted here is the notion and the round.

## 2.3 One body, many faces

> One body, many faces, is the method that runs through this whole course. A note by Chujia Kuang. The idea was distilled from more than twenty years of teaching and practice. It draws on the many-sided view of Dun Xinhui’s qimen work and on Li Jizhong’s habit of reading one hexagram for several questions. The difficulty in this subject is not remembering signs but getting free of the straight line that reads an officer as good news and a killing as bad.
> Yin and yang and the four images have already shown the method at its simplest: one qi of the great ultimate divides into yin and yang by its movement and rest, one body with two faces; the same waxing and waning gives four images by its point, one body with four faces.

In a chart the method goes a level higher. The mistake students make is to argue from one face: see the rob wealth and think only of wealth taken, see the seven killings and think only of disaster. But the rob wealth takes wealth and is also a partner; the seven killings attack the body and, once controlled, are the indirect officer. Anything is one whole, and in different dimensions it shows different faces. With that, the straight line goes: good is not always good, ill is not wholly ill, and everything lies in the angle and the combination.

Knowing this is easy; doing it is not. Every chart is read through three steps in order:

**Fix the dimension.** Without it the conclusion is confusion. Ask first which face is being read: temper, kin, health, work. The same direct officer shows public spirit when temper is read, the husband in the right place when marriage is read, and damage when the officer is clashed in health. This step belongs to lessons 3 to 9, the stems, the branches and the images of the ten gods.

**Weigh the force.** Get the force wrong and the whole reading is void. The same god, strong or weak, is a different thing: a strong direct officer is authority, a weak one an empty title; the wealth star with a strong self is riches, with a weak self a burden. This step belongs to lessons 14 and 15.

**Look at the combination.** Different combinations, different patterns. No god acts alone. The direct officer with the seal is officer and seal generating one another, fortunate; with the wounding officer it is the wounding officer meeting the officer, unfortunate. The same officer, matched one way or the other, is heaven and earth apart. This step belongs to lessons 10 to 12.

> The formula: one substance, different dimensions, different conclusions. The direct officer is the substance; in health it is pressure, in work it is power, in kin it is the husband. The three stand together and do not contradict one another.
> The *Ziping Zhenquan*: “Wealth, officer, seal and eating god are good, and are used along the grain; killing, wounding officer, rob wealth and blade are not good, and are used against it.” Along the grain where that is right, against it where that is right: the old writing already had the method. See also 2.4 of lesson 1, the two attributes of a chart.

## 2.4 The five phases

What the clash of the four images congeals is earth, and with wood, fire, metal and water that makes the five phases. They are not five substances but five states of qi in motion: wood rising and spreading outward, fire risen to the top, metal gathering downward and inward, water sunk to the bottom, and earth the hinge at which the four turn.

From the *Shangshu*, the Great Plan:

> 五行：一曰水，二曰火，三曰木，四曰金，五曰土。水曰润下，火曰炎上，木曰曲直，金曰从革，土爰稼穑。
> The five phases: the first water, the second fire, the third wood, the fourth metal, the fifth earth. Water is soaking and downward, fire blazing and upward, wood bending and straightening, metal following the mould, earth sowing and reaping.

Soaking and downward, blazing and upward, bending and straightening, following the mould, sowing and reaping: those five phrases are the core characters, and every later image of the phases grows from them.

### 2.4.1 The characters of the five phases

| Phase | Character | What falls under it | Season | Direction | Colour | Number | Organ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood | Bending and straightening | Growth, sprouting, spreading, ease | Spring | East | Green | 3, 8 | Liver |
| Fire | Blazing upward | Heat, rising, brightness, ardour | Summer | South | Red | 2, 7 | Heart |
| Earth | Sowing and reaping | Generating, carrying, receiving | Late summer | Centre | Yellow | 5, 10 | Spleen |
| Metal | Following the mould | Sinking, cutting, gathering, coolness | Autumn | West | White | 4, 9 | Lung |
| Water | Soaking downward | Moistening, descending, cold, flowing | Winter | North | Black | 1, 6 | Kidney |

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The five phases are not five substances but five ways that qi moves. Wood rises and spreads outward, fire has risen to the top, metal gathers down and in, water has sunk to the bottom, and earth is the hinge where the four turn. Learn them as movement, not as a list of what each one is.

The qi of the phases also shows in temperament, and the old Chinese summary of human nature runs: wood is benevolence, fire is propriety, earth is trust, metal is rightness, water is wisdom.

**Wood, benevolence:** upright, wanting to get on, a sense of duty, and stubborn. **Fire, propriety:** warm, inventive, good at expressing himself, and hasty. **Earth, trust:** reliable, steady, accommodating, patient, and slow to react. **Metal, rightness:** decisive, strong in execution, and hard. **Water, wisdom:** flexible, adaptable, perceptive, and given to suspicion.

Whichever phase is strongest in a chart gives the most conspicuous of these.

The numbers of the *Sanming Tonghui*, from the Hetu:

> 天一生水（北），地六成之 → 地二生火（南），天七成之 → 天三生木（东），地八成之 → 地四生金（西），天九成之 → 天五生土（中央），地十成之
> Heaven one gives birth to water in the north and earth six completes it; earth two gives birth to fire in the south and heaven seven completes it; heaven three gives birth to wood in the east and earth eight completes it; earth four gives birth to metal in the west and heaven nine completes it; heaven five gives birth to earth at the centre and earth ten completes it.

### 2.4.2 Generation and check

To generate is to help, promote, feed and encourage. What generates gives up energy and what is generated gains it, as a mother feeds a child or fuel feeds a fire. The round runs: wood burns into fire, fire leaves earth, earth gathers metal, metal melts into water, water moistens wood.

> 木生火 → 火生土 → 土生金 → 金生水 → 水生木（循环）
> Wood generates fire, fire earth, earth metal, metal water, water wood, and round again.

To check is to restrain, hold back, govern and prune. A check is not a destruction: it keeps a thing from going too far. Wood breaks through earth; earth banks water; water puts out fire; fire melts metal into a vessel; metal cuts wood into timber. Without the check the phases run loose, as a person without self-discipline comes to nothing.

> 木克土 → 土克水 → 水克火 → 火克金 → 金克木（循环）
> Wood checks earth, earth water, water fire, fire metal, metal wood, and round again.

From the *Yuanhai Ziping* on generation, check, control and transformation, neither of the two is a flat rule. A check is a restraint: kept to measure it holds a person inside the lines and lets him develop in order; carried too far it kills the initiative and leaves him stuck in the old way. Generation is a promotion: in measure it feeds and supports; in excess it breeds dependence and becomes a burden and a blockage. The direction depends on the balance of force. Five faces, one for each phase:

**Metal.**

> 金旺得火，方成器皿；金衰遇火，必见销熔。金能克木，木坚金缺；强金得水，方挫其锋；金赖土生，土多金埋。
> Metal at its strongest needs fire before it becomes a vessel; metal weak meeting fire is melted away. Metal checks wood, but wood too hard breaks the edge of the metal. Strong metal with water has its point blunted; metal relies on earth to be generated, and too much earth buries it.

**Wood.**

> 木旺得金，方成栋梁；木弱逢金，必为砍折。木能克土，土重木折；强木得火，方化其顽；木赖水生，水多木漂。
> Wood at its strongest needs metal before it becomes a beam; wood weak meeting metal is cut down. Wood checks earth, but earth too heavy snaps the root. Strong wood with fire has its stubbornness transformed; wood relies on water, and too much water floats it away.

**Fire.**

> 火旺得水，方成相济；火弱逢水，必为熄灭。火能克金，金多火熄；强火得土，方止其焰；火赖木生，木多火炽。
> Fire at its strongest needs water before the two come into balance; fire weak meeting water is put out. Fire checks metal, but much metal smothers it. Strong fire with earth has its flame checked; fire relies on wood, and too much wood makes it blaze.

**Water.**

> 水旺得土，方成池沼；水弱逢土，必为淤塞。水能克火，火炎水热；强水得木，方泄其势；水赖金生，金多水浊。
> Water at its fullest needs earth before it becomes a pool; water weak meeting earth is silted up. Water checks fire, but fire blazing turns the water warm. Strong water with wood has its force drawn off; water relies on metal, and too much metal muddies it.

**Earth.**

> 土旺得木，方能疏通；土衰遇木，必遭倾陷。土能克水，水多土流；强土得金，方制其害；土赖火生，火多土焦。
> Earth at its thickest needs wood before it is broken up and made workable; earth weak meeting wood is undermined. Earth checks water, but much water washes it away. Strong earth with metal has its harm checked; earth relies on fire, and too much fire scorches it.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. One body, many faces, and all of it lies between strong and weak. A check when you are strong helps you become a vessel; the same check when you are weak is snow on frost. What is to be learned is not who generates whom but how the balance of force decides which way the generation or the check will run.
> A study note. The working use of the phases — the strength of the day master, the choice of useful god, health — needs the ten gods (lessons 6 to 8) and the theory of strength and decline (lesson 12). What is wanted here is the characters and the two relations. The phases against the organs of the body are taken up in lesson 3, with the stems.

> **A word from the teacher.** You have now finished the most basic and the most important lesson in the course. Yin and yang and the five phases are the ground of the whole method: the stems, the branches, the ten gods and the question of strength all rest on this.
> If parts of it feel abstract, that is the ordinary course of learning. Put them down for now; every lesson that follows will help you read this one again. By lesson 3 something will come clear: so that is what jia wood as lesser yang means.
> Keep at it. You have taken the first and the hardest step.

## Summary of the lesson

1. Yin and yang: one body, each the root of the other, waxing and waning, turning into one another; and how they divide a chart.
2. The four images: the path from the ultimate through the two forms and the four images to the five phases; lesser yang better placed than greater yang; the special place of earth.
3. One body, many faces: one substance in different dimensions gives different conclusions; the three steps of fixing the dimension, weighing the force and looking at the combination.
4. The five phases: their characters, the relations of generation and check, and the full table of generation, check, control and transformation from the *Yuanhai Ziping*.

## Practice

1. Write out the four rules of yin and yang from memory.
2. Write out the cycles of generation and of check among the five phases from memory.
3. Learn the formula of one body, many faces and the three steps that go with it.
4. Think it over: how does that method free you from reading an officer as good news and a killing as bad?
