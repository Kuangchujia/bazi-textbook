# Lesson 3 · The Heavenly Stems and the Five Combinations

[简体中文](/textbook/L03/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L03/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L03/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L03/)

## 1. The images and characters of the ten stems

From the *Siyan Dubu*, on the ten stems:

> 十干化神，有影无形；无中生有，福禄难凭。
> The transformations of the ten stems have a shadow and no form; out of nothing they produce something, and yet fortune and stipend are hard to rely on.

The ten stems look like ten signs. They are the five phases in bodily form between heaven and earth: what has form is their image, what has none is their qi. Learning them means holding the image and seeing the qi.

### 1.1 Where the ten stems come from

The passage from the undifferentiated to the five phases runs:

> 天地一气，惟有动静，遂分阴阳。阴阳进一步演化，分出五行。天干即是五行之气在天道的具体表现，共十个，故谓十天干。
> The one qi of heaven and earth has only movement and rest, and so divides into yin and yang. Yin and yang evolve further and the five phases divide out. The stems are the concrete showing of the qi of the five phases in the way of heaven; there are ten of them, and so they are called the ten stems.

### 1.2 Images and characters

None of the ten names was given at random. Jia is the shell broken through; yi is the bent first shoot; bing is grown and showing its form; ding is full and formed; wu is thick and dense; ji is having form and a record; geng is firm and drawing in; xin is at the height and held back; ren is pregnancy and carrying; gui is closed away and waiting to sprout. The ten together trace one life from birth to the next sprouting.

Within one phase the yang stem and the yin stem differ in kind. Group by group:

**Jia wood, yang:** the great tree, tall and straight, with weight and presence; in the body it answers to the head and the gall. **Yi wood, yin:** flowers, grass and vines, soft and winding, adaptable and knowing how to go round; in the body, the shoulders and the liver.

**Bing fire, yang:** the fire of the sun, lighting heaven and earth, warm and open, generous. **Ding fire, yin:** the fire of a lamp, driving off the dark, fine and gentle, with a close-knit mind.

**Wu earth, yang:** city wall and high mountain, hard and thick, steady and reliable, and rather obstinate. **Ji earth, yin:** field and wet ground, soft and fertile, accommodating and able to bring forth.

**Geng metal, yang:** sword, axe and halberd, hard by nature, decisive and sharp, and apt to break from being too hard. **Xin metal, yin:** pearl, jade, gold and silver, warm in substance, fine and handsome, and apt to fret after perfection.

**Ren water, yang:** river, lake and sea, running wide, broad in what it holds, and apt to run loose without settling. **Gui water, yin:** rain, dew, frost and snow, moistening without a sound, fine and soaking, and apt to be sensitive and melancholy.

### 1.3 Image, number and principle

Before going further, fix the frame the subject thinks with, which is image, number and principle:

Image is how a thing shows: see jia wood and think of the great tree, the beam, the tall straight trunk. Number is the measure behind the image: the strength and decline of the phases, by which the balance of force is judged. Principle is the rule of how things run: the chain by which wood generates fire, and why jia wood generates bing fire.

A chart is read in that order: the image first, then the number, then the principle of generation, check, control and transformation. None of the three can be dropped.

## 2. Generation and check among the stems

Generation and check among the stems follow the same rule as among the phases: generation runs to the next in order, check skips one.

**Generation:** jia and yi wood, then bing and ding fire, then wu and ji earth, then geng and xin metal, then ren and gui water, and back to wood. What generates loses force and what is generated gains it: wood burns into fire, fire leaves earth, earth gathers metal, metal melts into water, water moistens wood.

**Check:** jia and yi wood check wu and ji earth; earth checks ren and gui water; water checks bing and ding fire; fire checks geng and xin metal; metal checks jia and yi wood, and round again. Both sides lose force, since a check is a consumption: whoever checks another spends himself.

> A way to remember. Going round the phases in order is generation; skipping one is check. Jia wood does not check bing fire — that is a relation of generation. It skips bing and checks wu earth.

## 3. The stems, the directions and the seasons

Jia and yi are wood, east, spring, when the ten thousand things come up. Bing and ding are fire, south, summer, the sun at its fiercest. Wu and ji are earth, at the centre, flourishing at the end of each season. Geng and xin are metal, west, autumn, cutting and gathering in. Ren and gui are water, north, winter, cold and closed away.

The direction and season of the day stem often point to where a person’s gifts lie and which way he does best to develop.

## 4. The stems and the body

The matching of stems to organs is the basis of reading health from a chart. The old mnemonic should be known by heart:

> 甲胆乙肝丙小肠，丁心戊胃己脾藏。庚是大肠辛为肺，壬是膀胱癸肾藏。
> Jia the gall, yi the liver, bing the small intestine; ding the heart, wu the stomach, ji stored in the spleen; geng the large intestine, xin the lung; ren the bladder, gui stored in the kidney.

## 5. The five combinations

### 5.1 The combinations

The five combinations come from the numbers of the Hetu, which sets out the making of things in five pairs, each pair one yin and one yang, one odd and one even, one before heaven and one after: joined, they complete.

> 天一生水，地六成之——一六共宗，水数也。甲为天干之首（一），己为天干之六。甲己合化土，因甲己居中宫（土位），合而化土。
> Heaven one gives birth to water and earth six completes it: one and six share a source, the number of water. Jia is the first of the stems and ji the sixth; jia and ji combine and transform into earth, because the pair occupies the central palace, the place of earth.

> 地二生火，天七成之——二七同道，火数也。乙为天干之二，庚为天干之七。乙庚合化金，因乙庚居西方（金位），合而化金。
> Earth two gives birth to fire and heaven seven completes it: two and seven walk the same road, the number of fire. Yi is the second stem and geng the seventh; they combine into metal, because the pair occupies the west, the place of metal.

> 天三生木，地八成之——三八为朋，木数也。丙为天干之三，辛为天干之八。丙辛合化水，因丙辛居北方（水位），合而化水。
> Heaven three gives birth to wood and earth eight completes it: three and eight are companions, the number of wood. Bing is the third stem and xin the eighth; they combine into water, because the pair occupies the north, the place of water.

> 地四生金，天九成之——四九为友，金数也。丁为天干之四，壬为天干之九。丁壬合化木，因丁壬居东方（木位），合而化木。
> Earth four gives birth to metal and heaven nine completes it: four and nine are friends, the number of metal. Ding is the fourth stem and ren the ninth; they combine into wood, because the pair occupies the east, the place of wood.

> 天五生土，地十成之——五十同途，土数也。戊为天干之五，癸为天干之十。戊癸合化火，因戊癸居南方（火位），合而化火。
> Heaven five gives birth to earth and earth ten completes it: five and ten go the same way, the number of earth. Wu is the fifth stem and gui the tenth; they combine into fire, because the pair occupies the south, the place of fire.

A second way to remember, by the marrying of a younger sister:

Jia wood checks wu earth, and wu earth marries its younger sister ji to jia: jia and ji combine into earth. Geng metal checks jia wood, and jia marries its younger sister yi to geng: yi and geng combine into metal. Bing fire checks geng metal, and geng marries its younger sister xin to bing: bing and xin combine into water. Ren water checks bing fire, and bing marries its younger sister ding to ren: ding and ren combine into wood. Wu earth checks ren water, and ren marries its younger sister gui to wu: wu and gui combine into fire.

Two yang stems checking one another are enemies; the one checked marries its younger sister to the one checking, turning enmity into kinship and settling the check by the marriage.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The five combinations are not hard. They are not five formulas to be memorised but five stories of a marriage. Jia checks wu, and the two should be enemies; wu marries its sister ji to jia, and the quarrel becomes a match. When you can look at jia and ji and smile, saying that brother jia has married sister ji, the five are already in your head.

### 5.2 Binding and transformation

A combination has two outcomes: the pair may bind without transforming, or may transform.

**Binding.** The pair are like lovers not yet married. Each keeps its own force, and each holds the other back and wears it down. When jia and ji bind, jia is still wood and ji still earth: jia wants to check ji and is held, and its force has nowhere to go.

> A central point from Chujia Kuang. There is one hard rule about binding in practice: a fortunate god that is bound does not deliver its good, and an unlucky god that is bound does not carry out its harm. Where a good god is held, the good thing does not fall to you; where a bad god is held, the bad thing is dissolved. Meeting a combination in a chart, do not judge fortune at once; see first whether what is bound is good or bad.

**Transformation.** The pair marry and produce, and a new phase comes out. Given the present state of the course, the beginner’s class uses binding only; transformation is taken up in the advanced course.

## Summary of the lesson

1. The yin and yang and the phases of the ten stems: jia, bing, wu, geng and ren are yang; yi, ding, ji, xin and gui are yin.
2. The frame of image, number and principle: image to see the thing, number to decide fortune, principle to find the root.
3. The images of the stems, yang and yin differing in kind: jia the great tree against yi the flower.
4. Generation and check: generation in order, check skipping one; the generator loses force and both sides of a check lose force.
5. Directions and seasons: jia and yi wood in the east and spring, bing and ding fire in the south and summer, geng and xin metal in the west and autumn, ren and gui water in the north and winter.
6. The stems and the organs: jia the gall, yi the liver, bing the small intestine, ding the heart, wu the stomach, ji the spleen, geng the large intestine, xin the lung, ren the bladder, gui the kidney.
7. The five combinations: jia and ji to earth, yi and geng to metal, bing and xin to water, ding and ren to wood, wu and gui to fire; the beginner’s class uses binding only.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: yin and yang of the stems

Which group is made up entirely of yang stems?

1. jia, bing, wu, geng, ren
2. jia, yi, bing, ding, wu
3. yi, ding, ji, xin, gui
4. bing, ding, wu, ji, geng

???+ "Answer"
    A. The yang stems are jia, bing, wu, geng and ren, at the odd positions; the yin stems are yi, ding, ji, xin and gui, at the even.

### Exercise 2: the images

How do the images of jia wood and yi wood differ?

1. Both are wood and there is no difference.
2. Jia is the flowering wood and yi the beam.
3. Jia is the beam, yang wood; yi is the flowering wood, yin wood.
4. Jia belongs to fire and yi to water.

???+ "Answer"
    C. Jia wood is the timber of beams, tall, straight, carrying weight and giving others a sense of security; yi wood is the flowering plant, soft and tough, adaptable and knowing how to go round. Yang stems are firm and active, yin stems yielding and passive. In practice, a day master of jia wood usually suits leading and deciding, and one of yi wood usually suits advising and coordinating.

### Exercise 3: check

By the relations of the phases, what do ren and gui water check?

1. jia and yi wood
2. bing and ding fire
3. wu and ji earth
4. geng and xin metal

???+ "Answer"
    B. Wood checks earth, earth checks water, water checks fire, fire checks metal, metal checks wood.

### Exercise 4: the organs

Which pairing of stem and organ is wrong?

1. jia, the gall
2. ding, the heart
3. geng, the large intestine
4. gui, the bladder

???+ "Answer"
    D. The pairings are ren the bladder and gui the kidney. Gui answers to the kidney, not the bladder. The mnemonic: jia the gall, yi the liver, bing the small intestine; ding the heart, wu the stomach, ji in the spleen; geng the large intestine, xin the lung; ren the bladder, gui in the kidney.

### Exercise 5: the combinations

What phase do jia and ji combine into?

1. wood
2. fire
3. earth
4. metal

???+ "Answer"
    C. Jia and ji to earth, yi and geng to metal, bing and xin to water, ding and ren to wood, wu and gui to fire. From the Hetu: one is jia, the birth number of water, and six is ji, the completion number of water; the pair goes to the centre, and earth is the result.
