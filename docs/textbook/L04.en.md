# Lesson 4 · The Earthly Branches

[简体中文](/textbook/L04/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L04/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L04/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L04/)

## 1. The phases and the polarity of the branches

The twelve branches fall into three kinds. Yin, shen, si and hai are the four births, the places of growth; zi, wu, mao and you are the four centres, the places of the peak; chen, xu, chou and wei are the four stores, the places of the tomb. Know these three and more than half of the branches is known.

### 1.1 The sequence

The order is zi, chou, yin, mao, chen, si, wu, wei, shen, you, xu, hai.

With their animals: zi the rat, chou the ox, yin the tiger, mao the hare, chen the dragon, si the snake, wu the horse, wei the goat, shen the monkey, you the fowl, xu the dog, hai the pig.

### 1.2 Polarity and phase

Polarity follows the stems: odd positions are yang. Zi, yin, chen, wu, shen and xu are yang branches; chou, mao, si, wei, you and hai are yin.

By phase: yin, mao and chen are wood, yin yang wood and mao yin wood; si, wu and wei are fire, si yang and wu yin; shen, you and xu are metal, shen yang and you yin; hai, zi and chou are water, hai yang and zi yin. Chen, xu, chou and wei are also earth, the earth of the four seasons.

> What this means in practice. The phase of a branch is not as pure as that of a stem. Many branches are mixed qi, holding a chief qi and a residual qi. That is where the branches are more complicated than the stems.

## 2. The branches, the seasons, the directions and the hours

From the *Yuanhai Ziping*, on which qi is in command through the month:

> 立春戊丙各七朝，一十四日甲木交。惊蛰甲木旺一旬，余下乙木有对冲。
> From the start of spring, wu earth and bing fire hold seven days each, and at fourteen days jia wood takes over. At the awakening of insects jia wood is strong for a ten-day period, and what remains is yi wood.

Within a month the qi of the phases is not spread evenly. In the first month, the yin month, wu earth is in command for the first seven days after the start of spring, bing fire for the next seven, and jia wood for the rest. That is where the hidden stems of yin come from: yin is a wood month, but the cold of early spring has not gone, and wu earth and bing fire go ahead to clear the way for the wood. The hidden stems are not an arbitrary list; they follow the solar terms as one qi hands over to the next.

*A textual point left open.* The received text of the *Qijie Ge* in the *Yuanhai Ziping* gives sixteen days to jia wood, seven and seven and sixteen making thirty; the line here gives fourteen, and seven and seven and fourteen make twenty-eight, which does not fill a thirty-day month. Both readings stand, and the text is left as it is.

### 2.1 Seasons and directions

Lesser yang wood, yin, mao and chen, governs spring and the east. Greater yang fire, si, wu and wei, governs summer and the south. Lesser yin metal, shen, you and xu, governs autumn and the west. Greater yin water, hai, zi and chou, governs winter and the north.

> 原文：少阳木见于寅，壮于卯，衰于辰。太阳火见于巳，壮于午，衰于未。少阴金见于申，壮于酉，衰于戌。太阴水见于亥，壮于子，衰于丑。
> Lesser yang wood appears in yin, is full in mao, declines in chen. Greater yang fire appears in si, is full in wu, declines in wei. Lesser yin metal appears in shen, is full in you, declines in xu. Greater yin water appears in hai, is full in zi, declines in chou.

The passage describes how the qi of each phase runs through the twelve months: it appears as the phase enters its season, reaches its height in the middle month, and declines at the end. Take wood: in the yin month the wood qi first shows, in mao it is at its strongest, and in chen the wood declines and earth is in command.

### 2.2 The double hours

The old division of the day into twelve double hours, two hours each:

Zi, 23:00 to 01:00, midnight. Chou, 01:00 to 03:00, the cock crowing. Yin, 03:00 to 05:00, dawn. Mao, 05:00 to 07:00, sunrise. Chen, 07:00 to 09:00, breakfast. Si, 09:00 to 11:00, the sun nearing the meridian. Wu, 11:00 to 13:00, noon. Wei, 13:00 to 15:00, the sun past the meridian. Shen, 15:00 to 17:00, late afternoon. You, 17:00 to 19:00, sunset. Xu, 19:00 to 21:00, dusk. Hai, 21:00 to 23:00, the hour of rest.

## 3. The four beginnings, the four centres, the four stores

By position and by solar term the twelve divide into three groups:

**The four beginnings**, the start of each season: yin, north-east, first month; si, south-east, fourth; shen, south-west, seventh; hai, north-west, tenth. These are the four places of growth.

**The four centres**: zi, due north, eleventh month; mao, due east, second; wu, due south, fifth; you, due west, eighth. These are the four places of the peak.

**The four stores**, the end of each season: chen, south-east, third month; wei, south-west, sixth; xu, north-west, ninth; chou, north-east, twelfth. These are the four tombs.

> 《四言独步》：”寅申巳亥，四生之局；用物身强，遇之发福。辰戌丑未，四库之神；人元三用，透旺为真。”
> Yin, shen, si and hai are the four places of growth; where what is used is strong in itself, meeting them brings fortune. Chen, xu, chou and wei are the four store gods; of the three hidden stems, the one that shows above and is strong is the true one.

## 4. The sixty pairs

Stems and branches combine with a yang stem on a yang branch and a yin stem on a yin branch, giving sixty pairs, the sixty of the cycle. Odd to odd, even to even: jia with zi, yi with chou, and so on, like gear teeth that cannot be offset.

> The cycle runs sixty and begins again. Each ten pairs make a decade, named for its first pair: the jia-zi decade, jia-zi to gui-you, which lacks xu and hai; the jia-xu decade, jia-xu to gui-wei, which lacks shen and you; the jia-shen decade, jia-shen to gui-si, which lacks wu and wei; the jia-wu decade, jia-wu to gui-mao, which lacks chen and si; the jia-chen decade, jia-chen to gui-chou, which lacks yin and mao; the jia-yin decade, jia-yin to gui-hai, which lacks zi and chou.
> Ten stems against ten branches always leaves two branches out, and what is left out is the void, which later lessons take up.
> A note by Chujia Kuang. The sixty pairs are the multiplication table of charting. There is no need to learn them all at the start, but the six decade heads must be known cold, because the void is reckoned from them: without them the void can never be found. One decade a day, six days, and it serves for life.

## 5. What the branches hide

### 5.1 The three origins

In this subject the stems are the origin of heaven, standing for the outward and visible force; the branches are the origin of earth, standing for what carries and roots; the stems hidden in the branches are the origin of man, standing for the inward and latent temper. The three come out of one pillar, and give the three levels of the analysis: the stem shows the outward manner, the branch the depth of the root, the hidden stem what is held in reserve.

Apart from zi, wu, mao and you, which are comparatively pure, the branches hide two or three stems, which is where the talk of mixed qi comes from. Yin is wood, and hides jia wood as its chief qi, bing fire as its middle qi and wu earth as its residual qi: yin is not only wood but holds fire and earth as well. That is why the branches are harder than the stems.

### 5.3 The rules behind the table

**The four centres**, zi, wu, mao and you, hold their own qi alone or the essence of it.

- zi: gui, pure water. mao: yi, pure wood. wu: ding and ji, fire chief. you: xin, pure metal.

**The four beginnings**, yin, shen, si and hai, run by generation: the chief qi in strength generates the middle qi, and the middle qi generates the residual.

- yin: jia, wood strong, generates fire, bing; bing strong generates earth, wu. Yin hides jia, bing, wu. si: bing generates earth, wu; wu generates metal, geng. Si hides bing, wu, geng. shen: geng generates water, ren; water and earth together, wu. Shen hides geng, ren, wu. hai: ren generates wood, jia. Hai hides ren, jia.

**The four corners**, chen, xu, chou and wei, take earth as chief qi, sitting at the centre as the qi of the seasonal turn.

- chen: wu, late spring; yi, the store of water; gui. Chen hides wu, yi, gui. wei: ji, late summer; ding, the store of wood; yi. Wei hides ji, ding, yi. xu: wu, late autumn; xin, the store of fire; ding. Xu hides wu, xin, ding. chou: ji, late winter; gui, the store of metal; xin. Chou hides ji, gui, xin.

*A textual point left open.* In the four lines just above, the “store” is attached to the second stem in each case: yi in chen, ding in wei, xin in xu, gui in chou. By the last stem in each branch the store would fall elsewhere: gui, water, in chen; yi, wood, in wei; ding, fire, in xu; xin, metal, in chou. The two readings differ by one place in all four. The text is left as it stands.

### 5.4 The mnemonic

> 子藏癸水在其中，丑中癸辛己土同。寅藏甲木和丙戊，卯中乙木独相逢。辰藏乙木兼戊癸，巳中庚金有丙戊。午藏丁火并己土，未中乙木加己丁。申藏戊土庚并壬，酉中辛金独丰隆。戌藏辛金及丁戊，亥中壬甲是真踪。
> Zi holds gui water within it; chou holds gui, xin with ji earth. Yin holds jia wood with bing and wu; mao meets yi wood alone. Chen holds yi wood together with wu and gui; si holds geng metal with bing and wu. Wu holds ding fire with ji earth; wei holds yi wood with ji and ding. Shen holds wu earth with geng and ren; you holds xin metal alone and in full. Xu holds xin metal with ding and wu; hai holds ren and jia, and there the true trace is.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The hidden stems are among the hardest things to memorise here and among the most important. My own order: the four beginnings first, by their rule of generation; then the four centres, which hold one stem each; the four stores last. Write them out once a day and within a week they come unbidden. Seeing a branch and not looking at what it hides is seeing a man’s face and not his mind.

## 6. Generation and check among the branches

The principle is the same as among the phases, but the branches hide stems and are therefore more complicated. Yin is not only wood but holds fire and earth; zi is water, and holds gui, pure water. The same relation of water generating wood does not work out quite the same when zi generates yin and when hai generates mao.

**Generation:** yin and mao wood generate si and wu fire; si and wu fire generate chen, xu, chou and wei earth; that earth generates shen and you metal; that metal generates hai and zi water; that water generates yin and mao wood.

**Check:** yin and mao wood check chen, xu, chou and wei; that earth checks hai and zi water; that water checks si and wu fire; that fire checks shen and you metal; that metal checks yin and mao wood.

> The heart of it is not the branch but what the branch hides. Which hidden stem is doing the work is what makes a reading of generation and check among branches worth anything.

## Summary of the lesson

1. The twelve branches, zi through hai, odd positions yang and even yin.
2. Seasons and directions: yin, mao, chen wood in the east and spring; si, wu, wei fire in the south and summer; shen, you, xu metal in the west and autumn; hai, zi, chou water in the north and winter.
3. The four beginnings, the four centres and the four stores: beginnings at the start of a season, centres at the middle of a direction, stores at the corners.
4. The sixty pairs: yang stem with yang branch, yin with yin, sixty in all.
5. What the branches hide: the three origins, single in the four centres, mixed elsewhere.
6. Generation and check among the branches, following the phases.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: polarity

Which group is made up entirely of yang branches?

1. zi, yin, chen, wu, shen, xu
2. zi, chou, yin, mao, chen, si
3. chou, mao, si, wei, you, hai
4. yin, mao, chen, si, wu, wei

???+ "Answer"
    A. The yang branches are zi, yin, chen, wu, shen and xu; the yin are chou, mao, si, wei, you and hai.

### Exercise 2: the double hours

What clock time does the wu hour answer to?

1. 09:00 to 11:00
2. 11:00 to 13:00
3. 13:00 to 15:00
4. 15:00 to 17:00

???+ "Answer"
    B. Zi 23 to 1, chou 1 to 3, yin 3 to 5, mao 5 to 7, chen 7 to 9, si 9 to 11, wu 11 to 13, wei 13 to 15, shen 15 to 17, you 17 to 19, xu 19 to 21, hai 21 to 23.

### Exercise 3: hidden stems

Which stems does the branch yin hide?

1. jia
2. jia and bing
3. jia, bing and wu
4. jia, yi and bing

???+ "Answer"
    C. Yin hides jia, bing and wu: jia wood as chief qi, bing fire as middle qi, since strong wood of itself generates fire, and wu earth as residual, since strong fire of itself generates earth.
