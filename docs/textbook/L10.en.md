# Lesson 10 · Combinations (I): the Frame of the Pattern

[简体中文](/textbook/L10/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L10/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L10/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L10/)

## 1. Lu, the blade and the month rob

### 1.1 Lu

Lu is the day stem standing at the office stage in a branch. In the month it is lu established, and strongest; in the day branch it is lu held alone; in the hour it is returning lu; in the year it is the lu of the year, in descending order of force. Lu is a fortunate god, steady and strong, and stands for the root by which a man moves forward in the world. In the month it cannot be used alone: wealth or officer has to be taken as the useful god besides. In practice, wherever lu stands, in any of the four pillars, the stem may be called strong.

### 1.2 The blade

The yang blade is a yang stem, jia, bing, wu, geng or ren, at the peak stage in a branch. The force of the phase is at its height and overreaches, and overreaching brings regret, so it is counted among the four unlucky gods and must be controlled before it is of use.

| Yang stem | Blade | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jia wood | mao | wood peaks in mao |
| bing fire | wu | fire peaks in wu |
| wu earth | wu | wu is argued with bing |
| geng metal | you | metal peaks in you |
| ren water | zi | water peaks in zi |

> The blade is a tiger: uncontrolled it injures the body, controlled it is authority. Officer and killing are the first choice to control it, and the pattern where killing and blade both show is often the boldest and the most capable of great things.

### 1.3 The month rob

The month rob is a yin stem, yi, ding, ji, xin or gui, at the peak stage in a branch. Its force is less than the yang blade and about that of lu, and so it is called the month rob. It needs no controlling; it is to be protected.

| Yin stem | Month rob | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yi wood | yin | wood peaks in yin, the yin running backward |
| ding fire | si | fire peaks in si |
| ji earth | si | ji is argued with ding |
| xin metal | shen | metal peaks in shen |
| gui water | hai | water peaks in hai |

> Order of force: blade, then lu, then rob. The blade is a fierce god, lu a steady fortunate one, and the month rob sits between them.
> **A distinction worth holding**, from twenty years of teaching, between the sheep blade and the yang blade. Beginners mix them up; one is a shensha and the other a ten god. The sheep blade is a shensha: for all ten stems, the peak branch is the sheep blade. The yang blade is a ten god and belongs to the five yang stems only. For those five the peak branch is both at once, as jia meeting mao is yang blade and sheep blade together; the yin stems have a sheep blade and no yang blade. The two are not to be run together.
> A note by Chujia Kuang. Lu, blade and rob are the first gate of pattern analysis. With lu established in the month the self is not weak, and lu still cannot be used alone: wealth or officer must be taken besides. With the yang blade in the month there is more care still. Among the wealthy patterns I have seen, killing and blade together are the boldest, and an uncontrolled blade is the likeliest to come to grief. Meeting a yang blade, the first question is whether officer or killing controls it: with control, authority is in hand; without, a tiger is out of the cage.

## 2. Rooting and showing

> The core of this lesson and the frame of the whole course. Rooting and showing are the first key to the strength of a ten god. The stems move and the branches are still, and between that movement and that stillness lies the difference between a god that has force and one that only looks as if it has. The notion runs through every lesson after this: every judgment of strength, of true and false, of fortune and ill, uses it. Easy to enter, hard to master, and this section should be read until it is reflex.

### 2.1 The terms

**Showing.** A stem hidden in a branch appearing again among the stems. Si hides bing, wu and geng, and if bing, wu or geng appears above, that is showing. Shown it is conspicuous; not shown it lies hidden.

**Rooting.** A stem having a root of its own phase in a branch. Jia above with jia hidden in yin below is rooted. With a root it is solid; without, empty.

**Hidden and not shown.** A branch hiding a stem that never appears above. Si hides bing fire, and if bing is nowhere in the four pillars, it is hidden and not shown. Its force waits inside until a cycle draws it out.

**Floating.** A stem with no root in the branches, a name without substance, its force cut in half.

**Cut at the foot.** The branch of the same pillar checking the stem: bing-zi, where zi water checks bing fire, and the stem is trapped.

**Capped.** The stem of the same pillar checking its branch: bing-shen, where bing fire checks shen metal, and the branch is held down.

### 2.2 One stem, four positions

Take bing fire and see how its force differs by the branch beneath it:

- bing-wu, rooted and showing: wu hides ding and ji, bing is rooted in wu, and the root is deep.
- bing-shen, capped: bing fire checks shen metal, the stem pressing the branch.
- bing-zi, cut at the foot: zi water checks bing fire, the stem held by the branch.
- bing-chen, floating: chen is earth and dulls the light of bing, whose root is not solid.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The stems move, and what shows above can be used; the branches are still, and a hidden stem that never shows has nowhere to act. The stem is the outward manner and the branch the inward support. A stem floating without a root is a flower in a mirror, a name without substance; a hidden stem that never shows is talent without occasion, wasted for a lifetime. Only shown and rooted at once, outward manner and inward root, does talent act and strength become useful.
> How stems and branches act on one another is the hardest problem in this art and the ground of every technique. Stillness and movement, empty and solid, carried and shown: none of it can be skipped.

## 3. How a chart is read

### 3.1 Three steps

**Step one: the combinations showing above, which fix the conflict.** The stems are the face of the chart, and which ten gods show there decides the main conflict of it. List every god on the stems, judge each for fortune, and see whether they control or transform one another.

> An example: ding fire shows in the month stem, ren water binds ding, and the peers are taking wealth. A cycle where food and wounding generate wealth, or officer and killing guard it, is good. Punishment between wei and xu in the branches, and a clash between zi and wu, decide the household and the health.

**Step two: the chief qi of the month branch, which fixes the setting.** Judge by the qi in command in the month, on the same principle: a fortunate god is used along the grain, an unlucky one controlled.

> An example: wu earth born in the wu month is a yang blade, an unlucky god that must be controlled. The seven killings should control the blade, with wealth feeding the killing as good and checking it as bad. Harmony between wu and wei, and between yin and hai, settles household and health.

**Step three: the state of the branches, which fixes the events.** Punishment, clash, check, harm and breaking among the branches decide what actually happens: punishment for lawsuits and injury, clash for upheaval and separation, harm for injury in the dark, breaking for damage and loss.

### 3.2 The rule of order

> Where there is an officer, argue the officer first; where there is none, argue the use. Weigh each pattern in full, and take the killing as the weightiest: turn the killing into authority and there is no need to fret about the use.
> Officer and killing first, with the direct officer above the rest; with neither, the other gods; a fortunate god used along the grain and an unlucky one controlled against it.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The three steps are the steadiest frame I have found in twenty years: fix the conflict, fix the setting, fix the events, and the skeleton of the chart is up. Many students stick at the first step, looking at a crowd of gods and not knowing where to begin. Hold one line: where there is an officer, argue the officer first. The direct officer is the main line of a life, and a clean officer makes the whole chart clean.

## 4. The four fortunate and the four unlucky

> From the *Xiji Pian*: where officer and killing are evenly matched, keep what is liked and cast out what is disliked. From the *Siyan Dubu*: officer and killing met together are useful when controlled, and a cycle into the peak meeting them is not ill. Fortune and ill are not fixed labels; the control and the transformation are what matter.

The division is not absolute. Officer and killing together, controlled, are authority; uncontrolled, disaster.

### 4.1 The four fortunate

They stand for the four chief sources of good in a life: work, learning, health, money. When in charge they are to be protected, and not lightly checked or taken.

| God | What it stands for | What shows when of use | Its enemy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| direct officer | work and honour; children in a man’s chart, the husband in a woman’s | recognition and support from superiors | wounding officer |
| seal | household and parents, diplomas, property | help from elders, a strong household | wealth |
| eating god | health and wisdom; children in a woman’s chart; years and fortune | talent shown, recognition from juniors | owl |
| wealth | money; the wife in a man’s chart; the father | help from the wife’s family | peers |

### 4.2 The four unlucky

They stand for the forces of restraint, seizure and damage. The ill is not absolute: controlled they become authority, uncontrolled disaster cannot be avoided.

| God | What it stands for | How to control it | The harm uncontrolled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| seven killings | petty men and trouble, injury, a woman’s lover | the eating god to control, the seal to transform | attacking the body, disaster |
| yang blade, rob wealth | thieves and rivals, taking wealth | officer or killing to control the blade | loss and injury |
| wounding officer | stirring trouble, arrogance and rebellion | the seal to control it | damaging the officer and defying superiors |
| owl | checking the eating god, blocking food from generating wealth | wealth to control the owl | taking the food, losing fortune |

> A note by Chujia Kuang. The four and the four are the doctrine of one body, many faces exactly: the direct officer with wealth and seal is a noble pattern, with wounding officer and killing a disaster; the seven killings with the eating god to control them are authority, without it disaster. One god, and fortune or ill turns on whether it is controlled. Fortunate gods want protection, unlucky ones control, and both are a matter of the pattern.

## Summary of the lesson

1. Lu, blade and rob: lu the office stage, the blade the peak of a yang stem, the month rob the peak of a yin stem, with force blade over lu over rob. The blade needs control, lu protection, and the month rob is like lu.
2. Rooting and showing: rooted and shown means force and a name; floating means a name without force; hidden and not shown, force without a name.
3. Reading a chart: the combinations above fix the conflict, the month qi fixes the setting, the branches fix the events, and officer and killing come first.
4. The four fortunate: direct officer, seal, eating god, wealth, to be protected and not checked.
5. The four unlucky: seven killings, yang blade, wounding officer, owl, to be controlled before they are authority.
6. Their matchings, food controlling the killing and generating wealth, wealth generating the officer, the officer with wealth and seal, the seal fed by the officer: see lesson 11.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: lu, blade, rob

Which is strongest: the blade, lu or the month rob?

1. blade, then lu, then rob
2. lu, then blade, then rob
3. rob, then lu, then blade
4. blade, then rob, then lu

???+ "Answer"
    A. The blade is a yang stem at the peak, fierce in force; lu is the office stage, steady and strong; the month rob is a yin stem at the peak, weaker than the blade and about equal to lu.

### Exercise 2: rooting

What is a stem with no root in the branches called?

1. rooted
2. floating
3. capped
4. cut at the foot

???+ "Answer"
    B. Floating, or showing without substance, means no force. Cut at the foot is a branch checking its stem, as bing-zi; capped is a stem checking its branch, as bing-shen.

### Exercise 3: the four fortunate

Which group is made up entirely of the four fortunate gods?

1. direct officer, seven killings, eating god, wealth
2. direct officer, seal, eating god, wealth
3. seven killings, wounding officer, owl, yang blade
4. direct officer, direct wealth, direct seal, wounding officer

???+ "Answer"
    B. The four fortunate are the direct officer, the seal, the eating god and wealth; the four unlucky are the seven killings, the yang blade or rob wealth, the wounding officer and the owl.
