# Lesson 6 · Setting Out a Chart

[简体中文](/textbook/L06/) ｜ [繁體中文](/zh-hant/textbook/L06/) ｜ **English** ｜ [日本語](/ja/textbook/L06/) ｜ [한국어](/ko/textbook/L06/)

## 1. Counting years by stem and branch

### 1.1 The calendar behind it

Counting by stem and branch belongs to the Chinese lunisolar calendar. Three kinds of calendar have to be told apart first:

| Calendar | Basis | Example | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solar | The earth round the sun | The Gregorian | A fixed 365-day year; months unrelated to the moon |
| Lunar | The moon round the earth | The Hijri | Fixed 29 or 30 day months; the year drifts out of the seasons |
| Lunisolar | Sun and moon together | The Chinese | Months by new moon, years by the tropical year, leap months to reconcile |

The stems and branches cycle inside that lunisolar calendar, sixty years to a round. What must be fixed in mind is that the year pillar is set neither at 1 January nor at the first day of the lunar year, but at the start of spring in the stem-branch calendar.

> Setting the year pillar: look up the stem and branch of the birth year in the almanac, then establish whether the birth falls before or after the start of spring. Before it, the previous year’s pair; after it, the current year’s.

### 1.2 Why the first month is the yin month

That the chart takes the yin month as the first is not a fact of nature. It is the result of an edict.

The pre-Qin states kept the custom of changing the first month: each dynasty declared afresh where the year began. Xia began at yin, Shang at chou, Zhou at zi, Qin at hai. The first month moved with each house until, in the first year of the Taichu era, 104 BC, the Han court promulgated the Taichu calendar and restored the Xia reckoning at yin. For two thousand years since, the first month has been the yin month.

> One thing to remember: the month pillar begins at the yin month, and this is where that comes from. The *Liji* speaks of setting the weights and measures, examining the documents and correcting the first month: the calendar was never purely astronomy, but also politics.

### 1.3 The moment of the solar term

The start of spring is not a day but a particular second of a particular day. The moment decides both the year pillar and the month pillar.

- Born before the moment: the previous term’s pair still applies.
- Born after it: the current term’s pair applies.

> An example, the start of spring in 2021, 3 February at 22:58:39. Born a second before it, 22:58:38: the year pillar is geng-zi and the month pillar ji-chou, giving geng-zi, ji-chou, ren-wu, xin-hai. Born eleven seconds after, 22:58:50: the year pillar is xin-chou and the month geng-yin, giving xin-chou, geng-yin, ren-wu, xin-hai. One second, and the year pillar differs. That is why there is no room for error here.

## 2. Counting months

### 2.1 The solar terms are the only boundary

The month pillar is set by the “nodes” among the twenty-four solar terms, not by the first of the lunar month. The twenty-four divide into nodes and breaths: a node opens the month (start of spring, awakening of insects, clear and bright, start of summer, grain in ear, minor heat, start of autumn, white dew, cold dew, start of winter, major snow, minor cold), and a breath falls in the middle (rain water, spring equinox, grain rain and the rest). The twelve nodes are the boundaries of the month pillar.

> One rule to hold: when the start of spring arrives, the yin month has begun, whatever day of the lunar month it falls on. Even on the twenty-eighth of the twelfth month, spring once begun, the month pillar changes.

### 2.2 The twelve months

The table gives the month, its branch, the node, the breath and the usual solar date. Set the month pillar by it:

| Month | Branch | Node | Breath | Solar date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | yin | start of spring | rain water | 4 or 5 February |
| Second | mao | awakening of insects | spring equinox | 5 or 6 March |
| Third | chen | clear and bright | grain rain | 4 or 5 April |
| Fourth | si | start of summer | grain full | 5 or 6 May |
| Fifth | wu | grain in ear | summer solstice | 5 or 6 June |
| Sixth | wei | minor heat | major heat | 7 or 8 July |
| Seventh | shen | start of autumn | end of heat | 7 or 8 August |
| Eighth | you | white dew | autumn equinox | 7 or 8 September |
| Ninth | xu | cold dew | frost descends | 8 or 9 October |
| Tenth | hai | start of winter | minor snow | 7 or 8 November |
| Eleventh | zi | major snow | winter solstice | 7 or 8 December |
| Twelfth | chou | minor cold | major cold | 5 or 6 January |

At the start, learn the solar term song by heart. Four lines of seven characters carry the whole year:

> 春雨惊春清谷天，夏满芒夏暑相连。秋处露秋寒霜降，冬雪雪冬小大寒。
> Spring rain, awakening, clear and bright, grain rain; summer full, grain in ear, summer heat joined on; autumn begins, heat ends, white dew, cold dew, frost; winter snow, minor snow, winter, minor and major cold.

### 2.3 The month stem: the five tigers

The solar term table settles the month branch; the stem still has to be worked out. The rule is simple: the year stem decides the stem of the first month, and from there the stems run in order.

| Year stem | Stem of the first month | The old line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jia, ji | bing | In a jia or ji year, bing leads |
| yi, geng | wu | In a yi or geng year, wu is at the head |
| bing, xin | geng | In a bing or xin year, look for geng at the start |
| ding, ren | ren | In a ding or ren year, ren takes its place and runs on |
| wu, gui | jia | For wu or gui, seek on the jia-yin |

> How to use it: see which group the year stem falls in and fix the first month’s stem. In a jia or ji year the first month is bing-yin, the second ding-mao, the third wu-chen, and so on. Month pillar is month stem plus month branch, two steps and done.

## 3. Counting days

### 3.1 The day pillar is the odd one out

The year pillar fixes the root of the family, the month the parents and the surroundings, the hour the children and later life. The day pillar fixes the person himself. Its stem is the day master, the centre of the whole chart, and every ten god, every generation and check, every judgment of strength turns on it. One character wrong in the day pillar and the chart is another chart.

It is also the one pillar with no rule to compute it. The year has the start of spring, the month has the nodes, the hour has the twelve double hours: all of them can be reasoned to. The day cannot, because the solar calendar has leap years and long and short months while the stem-branch day runs in one unbroken sixty-day cycle, and the two rhythms do not answer to a single formula over all years. For that reason the count of days by stem and branch ran without a break from the Spring and Autumn period to the end of the Qing, more than 2,600 years. After 1912, when the Republic adopted the Western year, the count was formally abolished and has gone on in popular use ever since — some 2,700 years in all, the longest unbroken count of days in the world.

### 3.2 How to set it

**The recommended way: the almanac.** A modern chart is always set by looking the day up. Enter the solar birth date and read the pair for that day: the most accurate and the quickest, and what everyone does.

**The advanced way: the running-moon method.** A set of formulae by which blind practitioners reckon the day pillar with no tools, requiring a great many base numbers, month codes and day codes and a good deal of arithmetic. The cost of learning it is high and the beginner has no need of it: with an almanac in hand there is no call for the long way round.

> **One warning.** The day pillar is the only one of the four that cannot be reasoned out by a simple rule. Check it once set: an error in the day pillar drags the hour pillar with it and the whole chart is void.

## 4. Counting hours

### 4.1 The twelve double hours

The old division of the day into twelve double hours. Match the birth time and the hour branch is fixed:

| Double hour | Branch | Clock time | Old name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| zi | zi | 23:00 to 01:00 | midnight |
| chou | chou | 01:00 to 03:00 | cock crow |
| yin | yin | 03:00 to 05:00 | dawn |
| mao | mao | 05:00 to 07:00 | sunrise |
| chen | chen | 07:00 to 09:00 | breakfast |
| si | si | 09:00 to 11:00 | the sun nearing the meridian |
| wu | wu | 11:00 to 13:00 | noon |
| wei | wei | 13:00 to 15:00 | the sun past the meridian |
| shen | shen | 15:00 to 17:00 | late afternoon |
| you | you | 17:00 to 19:00 | sunset |
| xu | xu | 19:00 to 21:00 | dusk |
| hai | hai | 21:00 to 23:00 | the hour of rest |

### 4.2 The hour stem: the five rats

The branch is fixed by the clock; the stem comes from the day stem, on the same plan as the five tigers. The day stem decides the stem of the zi hour, and the stems run on in order to the birth hour:

| Day stem | Stem of the zi hour | The old line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jia, ji | jia | For jia and ji, add jia |
| yi, geng | bing | For yi and geng, bing begins |
| bing, xin | wu | For bing and xin, start from wu |
| ding, ren | geng | For ding and ren, geng sits at zi |
| wu, gui | ren | For wu and gui, ren-zi is the true road |

> How to use it: see which group the day stem falls in and fix the zi hour. In a jia or ji day the zi hour is jia-zi, then chou is yi-chou, yin bing-yin, and so on to the birth hour. Hour pillar is hour stem plus hour branch.
> **Daylight saving and true solar time.** For births between 1986 and 1991, subtract an hour to recover standard time before setting the hour pillar. The birthplace differs from 120 degrees east by four minutes for every degree, and at the boundary between two double hours the correction must be made. See the practice notes in lesson 1.

### 4.3 How the zi hour is handled

Alone among the twelve, the zi hour straddles two days, 23:00 to 01:00, and which day it belongs to is the commonest trap in setting a chart. Two treatments are in use.

**The simplified rule: from 23:00 the next day’s date is taken for both the day pillar and the hour pillar.**

| Period | Hour pillar | Day pillar | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23:00 to 01:00 | the zi hour of the next day | the next day | from 23:00 a new day has begun |

> An example: born 29 May 2020 at 23:00 gives the day gui-hai, that of 30 May, and the hour ren-zi.

Why the simplified rule is preferred:

1. It is one rule throughout, with no argument over an early and a late zi hour.
2. It is hard to get wrong. Mixing up the day pillar is the commonest student error, and the simplified rule removes it.
3. Thirty years of practice shows it accurate enough for ordinary reading.

> A note by Chujia Kuang. I start every student on the simplified rule. Lay the foundation first; the traditional treatment can come later, when a disputed case turns up. In most practice the two give very nearly the same answer.

**The traditional treatment** takes midnight, 00:00, as the boundary and splits the zi hour in two:

| Period | Name | Hour pillar | Day pillar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23:00 to 00:00 | late zi | the zi hour of the following day | the previous day |
| 00:00 to 01:00 | early zi | the zi hour of the current day | the current day |

> The same case, born 29 May 2020 at 23:00. Simplified: day gui-hai, hour ren-zi. Traditional: day ren-xu, hour ren-zi, since 23:00 to 00:00 is the late zi hour and the day pillar stays with the 29th.
> The two differ by a day in the day pillar, and the ten gods and the starting age of the cycles differ with it. Decide which rule is being used before setting the chart, and keep to it.

## 5. Setting out a chart, a full example

From the *Yuanhai Ziping*, on the frame of the eight characters:

> 以日为主，以年为根，以月为苗，以日为花，以时为果。
> Take the day as the master, the year as the root, the month as the shoot, the day as the flower and the hour as the fruit.

Each pillar has its part: the year is the root, the month the growth, the day the flowering, the hour the fruit. The four sections above have set out the rules for each; this one runs them as a single line.

The four in brief:

- Year: the start of spring is the boundary; before it the previous year’s pair, after it the current.
- Month: the node is the boundary, from the table in 2.2; the stem by the five tigers.
- Day: from the almanac.
- Hour: the branch by the table in 4.1, the stem by the five rats, with attention to the zi hour and to true solar time.

### 5.1 The worked case

Born 8 October 2021 at 20:00. Four steps.

**Step one, the year.** 2021 is xin-chou. The start of spring that year fell on 3 February and October is well past it, so the year pillar is xin-chou. No dispute.

**Step two, the month.** Which term does 8 October fall under? Cold dew falls about that date; in 2021 the exact moment was 8 October at 09:39. The birth is at 20:00, after it, so the month branch is xu. The stem by the five tigers: the year is xin, and in a xin year the first month is geng-yin; count to the ninth month and it is wu-xu. Month pillar: wu-xu.

**Step three, the day.** The almanac gives 8 October 2021 as ji-chou. Day pillar: ji-chou. The easiest of the four: look it up.

**Step four, the hour.** 20:00 is the xu hour, so the branch is xu. The stem by the five rats: the day stem is ji, and the line for ji is that jia is added, so the zi hour is jia-zi. Count on: chou yi-chou, yin bing-yin, and at xu it is jia-xu. Hour pillar: jia-xu.

Collected:

| Step | What was done | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year | 2021 is xin-chou; past the start of spring, 3 February | xin-chou |
| Month | after cold dew at 09:39 the month is xu; xin year by the five tigers | wu-xu |
| Day | almanac, 8 October 2021 | ji-chou |
| Hour | 20:00 is the xu hour; ji day by the five rats gives jia-xu | jia-xu |

> The four pillars: xin-chou, wu-xu, ji-chou, jia-xu.

Check the four: the year boundary at the start of spring is right; the month boundary at the node is right and the five tigers counted correctly; the day from the almanac is right; the hour branch is right and the five rats counted correctly. With the four checked, the next stage can begin.

## 6. Setting out the luck cycles

From the *Wuyan Dubu*, on the cycles:

> 运行十载数，上下五年分；先看流年岁，深知来往旬。
> A cycle runs ten years, counted five above and five below; look first at the year, and know the decades coming and going.

A cycle changes every ten years, each pair taking its turn. The four pillars are fate, the settings from the factory; the cycles are luck, the road afterwards. Good luck is worth more than good fate: a good chart through thirty years of bad cycles gets nowhere, and an ordinary chart on a clear road goes far.

### 6.1 Forward or backward

The cycles start from the month pillar and run by sex and by the polarity of the year stem:

| Year and sex | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| yang year, man; yin year, woman | forward, counting on from the month pillar |
| yin year, man; yang year, woman | backward, counting back from the month pillar |

> A yang year has jia, bing, wu, geng or ren as its stem; a yin year yi, ding, ji, xin or gui.

Once the first cycle is set, the rest follow in the same direction: forward stays forward, backward stays backward, and the direction does not change midway.

### 6.2 The starting age

The cycles have to be given a starting age as well as a sequence.

The method: count from the birth day forward, for a yang-year man or a yin-year woman, or backward, for a yin-year man or a yang-year woman, to the next or the previous node, and see how many days intervene. Count to a node, not to a breath.

- Three days make one year.
- One day makes four months.
- One double hour makes ten days.

> Three days or less is counted as starting at one year old.

### 6.3 A worked case

Using xin-chou, wu-xu, ji-chou, jia-xu:

| Sex | Direction | The count | Starts at | First cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| woman, yin year | forward | 8 October to the next node, the start of winter on 7 November, is 30 days, divided by 3 | 10 | ji-hai |
| man, yin year | backward | 8 October back to the previous node, cold dew at 09:39 the same day, is about 0 days | 1 | ding-you |

> In this case a woman starting at ten means the small cycles carry the years from birth to nine, as below; a man starting at one is in his first cycle, ding-you, from his second year.

## 7. The small cycles

### 7.1 What they are

The small cycles fill the gap. Before a child enters the first great cycle, the small cycles carry the year-by-year judgment. A great cycle changes every ten years, a small one every year, on the same rules but from a different starting point: the hour pillar rather than the month.

### 7.2 The rule

From the hour pillar, in the direction the great cycles take: forward for a yang-year man or yin-year woman, backward for a yin-year man or yang-year woman, changing every year rather than every ten.

> The small cycles are used only before the first great cycle begins. Once it begins they are dropped.

### 7.3 Examples

A woman’s chart, xin-chou, wu-xu, ji-chou, jia-xu. Xin is a yin year and she is a woman, so forward. From the hour pillar jia-xu: at one, yi-hai; at two, bing-zi; at three, ding-chou; and so on to nine. At ten the first great cycle, ji-hai, begins and the small cycles stop.

A man’s chart, ren-chen, gui-chou, ren-shen, geng-shen. Ren is a yang year and he is a man, so forward. From geng-shen: at one, xin-you; at two, ren-xu; at three, gui-hai; and so on to nine. At ten the first great cycle, jia-yin, begins and the small cycles stop.

> A word from the teacher. The first five lessons are the grounding. A pianist begins with finger exercises and a martial artist with standing post; setting the chart is the standing post of this subject. Each pillar has its boundary: the year at the start of spring, the month at the node, the day from the almanac, the hour fixed by the branch. When it is set, check the three places where errors come: the year boundary, the month node, and the early and late zi hour.
> Slowing down is how you go faster. Before the next lesson, set fifty charts by hand. I ask it of every student.

## A checklist of common errors

Six places where charts go wrong, gone over once the four pillars are set.

| No. | The trap | How it goes wrong | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the year boundary | born on the first of the lunar month, and the year pillar taken as changed | the year turns at the start of spring |
| 2 | the month node | no check of the moment, a branch taken at random | check the almanac to the minute; the next month begins only past the node |
| 3 | the zi hour | born at 23:00, and no knowing which day the pillar belongs to | the simplified rule: from 23:00 the day pillar is the next day |
| 4 | daylight saving | born 1986 to 1991, the clock time used as it stands | subtract an hour to standard time before setting the hour |
| 5 | true solar time | a western longitude read off Beijing time | four minutes a degree from 120 east; always correct at a double-hour boundary |
| 6 | the two mnemonics | the tigers and the rats mixed up, month and hour stems reversed | tiger for the year into months, rat for the day into hours |

> The mnemonic: year at the start of spring, month at the node, day from the almanac, hour by the branch. Four boundaries, and not one of them may be missed.

## Summary of the lesson

1. Years: the start of spring is the boundary, and the moment is exact to the second.
2. Months: the node opens the month, and the stem comes by the five tigers.
3. Days: from the almanac; the one pillar with no rule to compute it.
4. Hours: the branch fixed, the stem by the five rats, with the zi hour and daylight saving and true solar time watched.
5. The whole line: year, month, day, hour, with a full worked case.
6. The great cycles: forward for a yang-year man or yin-year woman, backward for the others; three days to a year.
7. The small cycles: from the hour pillar, one a year, before the first great cycle.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: the year pillar

A person is born on 1 February 2026, the start of spring that year falling on 4 February. What is the year pillar?

1. bing-wu
2. yi-si
3. jia-chen
4. ding-wei

???+ "Answer"
    B. The year turns at the start of spring, not at the lunar new year. 1 February is before it, so the previous year stands, and 2025 is yi-si.

### Exercise 2: the month pillar

In a jia or ji year, what is the month pillar of the first month?

1. bing-yin
2. wu-yin
3. geng-yin
4. ren-yin

???+ "Answer"
    A. In a jia or ji year, bing leads. Yi and geng give wu-yin, bing and xin geng-yin, ding and ren ren-yin, wu and gui jia-yin.

### Exercise 3: the hour pillar

For someone born on a jia or ji day, what is the zi hour?

1. jia-zi
2. bing-zi
3. wu-zi
4. geng-zi

???+ "Answer"
    A. For jia and ji, add jia, so the zi hour is jia-zi. Yi and geng give bing-zi, bing and xin wu-zi, ding and ren geng-zi, wu and gui ren-zi.

### Exercise 4: the whole chart

What are the four pillars for a birth on 8 October 2021 at 20:00?

1. xin-chou, ding-you, ji-chou, jia-xu
2. xin-chou, wu-xu, ji-chou, jia-xu
3. xin-chou, ji-hai, wu-zi, jia-xu
4. geng-zi, wu-xu, ji-chou, jia-xu

???+ "Answer"
    B. The year is xin-chou; 20:00 on 8 October 2021 is past cold dew at 09:39, so the month is wu-xu; the almanac gives the day ji-chou; 20:00 is the xu hour, and on a ji day the five rats count from jia-zi to jia-xu.

### Exercise 5: the cycles

Someone born in a xin-chou year, in the bing-shen month, is a woman. Do the great cycles run forward or backward?

1. forward, yang year with a man or yin year with a woman
2. backward, yang year with a man or yin year with a woman
3. forward, yin year with a man or yang year with a woman
4. backward, yin year with a man or yang year with a woman

???+ "Answer"
    A. Xin is a yin stem and she is a woman, so yin year with a woman: forward.
